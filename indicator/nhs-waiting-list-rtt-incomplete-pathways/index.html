About this figure

  • A stock, not a flow — the number of referral-to-treatment pathways still open at the end of the month. Each point is the December figure, except the latest year, which is a partial year taken at the most recent month published
  • Counts pathways, not people. One patient waiting for two treatments counts twice; NHS England estimates the number of unique patients is roughly 10–15% lower
  • The collection began in 2007; the steep fall through 2008 is real — trusts were clearing long waits ahead of the 18-week target that came in at the end of that year
  • Includes NHS England's estimates for trusts that did not submit data in a given month, so the series is not distorted by missing returns
  • Consultant-led elective care only. It does not include A&E, cancer, mental health or community waits

Breaks in the series

  • 2013: Sexual health removed. From April 2013 consultant-led sexual health services were no longer commissioned by the NHS and dropped out of RTT reporting. These were mostly short, week-long pathways
  • 2020: COVID-19. Referrals collapsed during the pandemic and elective activity was paused, so the list rose sharply afterwards as delayed demand returned
  • 2024: Community services removed. From February 2024 data, community service pathways should no longer be reported in RTT data, which took a small number of pathways off the list. Figures before and after are not on quite the same basis
