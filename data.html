Explorer Verify

Every number, and where it came from. Each indicator with its source release, the exact endpoint its values were pulled from, when, and every value as fetched.

Each name opens that indicator’s own page. Or browse by topic: Work, pay & living standards · Economy & public finances · Housing · Education · Health · Crime & justice · Transport · Environment & energy · Population & migration · all, A–Z

The three series every transform divides by: population for per person, the GDP deflator for real terms, nominal GDP for % of GDP. If a transformed number looks wrong, check these first.

Four steps, none of which require taking our word for anything.

  1. Open the indicator and hit its API endpoint — the same address the pipeline called, at the source, not a copy we host.
  2. Compare the year you care about with the raw values in the expanded row. Monthly and quarterly sources become one value a year — an average, the last month of a stock, or a total — and the expanded row says which.
  3. Read the source release for context. Definitions, revisions and footnotes live there, and the caveat badges on each row point at what to look for.
  4. Or reproduce the whole thing. Clone the repository and run python ingest/ingest.py --dry-run: it fetches everything fresh and reports what it got. Every transform happens in your browser, from the values here.

Found a mismatch? Sources revise their own history, which is normal for official statistics, and the weekly refresh picks that up. Anything else is a bug, and worth telling us about.
