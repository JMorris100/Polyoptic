Have rents outrun pay in the UK?

Not quite, measured from 2015: private rents are 4% behind average pay, having been 8% behind at the widest, in 2022.

Source: ONS Price Index of Private Rents; ONS Average Weekly Earnings · Open Government Licence v3.0 · a year still in progress is left out

The claim

Rents are rising faster than wages.

The numbers

Rebased to 2015 = 100, private rents reached 145 by 2025 and average pay 151: private rents +45%, average pay +51%. Average pay rose faster.

What it actually shows

An index of rents on new and existing tenancies is not the same as what a mover pays. Advertised rents for new lets have risen faster than this index over the same years, because existing tenancies reprice slowly. So this understates the change facing someone moving today, and overstates it for someone who has not moved.

Why a line chart

Rents and pay are in different units; rebasing both to 2015 = 100, the first year the rents index covers, makes their growth comparable on one axis.

Read it carefully

The index you pick changes the answer. This page used ONS’s old Index of Private Housing Rental Prices until it stopped in March 2025. Over 2015 to 2024, the years both cover, that index had rents up 23%; its replacement, the Price Index of Private Rents used here, has them up 37%. PIPR is published as official statistics in development, so ONS may still revise it, and it is UK-wide, which hides a wide spread between London and everywhere else.
