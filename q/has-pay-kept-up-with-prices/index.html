Has pay kept up with prices in the UK?

Only just, measured from 2008: average pay is 5% ahead of prices, after trailing prices every year from 2009 to 2020. From 2000, it is 24% ahead.

Source: ONS Average Weekly Earnings; ONS · Open Government Licence v3.0 · a year still in progress is left out

The claim

Pay has not kept up with the cost of living.

The numbers

Rebased to 2008 = 100, average pay reached 167 by 2025 and prices 160: average pay +67%, prices +60%. Average pay rose faster. Measured from 2000 instead, average pay +133% and prices +88%, so average pay ended 24% ahead of prices: the start year changes the answer.

What it actually shows

This is the whole-economy average, so it answers the question for the economy and not for any particular person. A mean is pulled up by high earners; it moves when the mix of who is employed changes, not only when anyone gets a rise.

And it is cumulative from the start year. From 2008, the year most people mean, pay spent twelve years behind prices; from 2000 it was ahead the whole time. Both are true, which is why both are here.

Why a line chart

Two growth rates from one start: rebasing both to 100 in the same year shows which pulled ahead, and when. The start year decides the answer, so the page gives the answer from a second one too.

Read it carefully

CPIH includes owner-occupiers’ housing costs, which CPI does not, and runs below CPI in most periods — the choice of price index changes the answer. Earnings here are total pay including bonuses, seasonally adjusted.
