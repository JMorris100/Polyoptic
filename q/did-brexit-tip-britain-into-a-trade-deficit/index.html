Did Brexit tip Britain into a trade deficit?

No: the UK ran a trade deficit in every one of the 18 years from 1998 to 2015, before the referendum.

The numbers

The UK ran a trade deficit in every one of the 18 years from 1998 to 2015, before the referendum, and in nine of the 10 years since. It averaged 1.5% of GDP a year before the vote and 0.8% after. The deficit is decades old.

Why a columns chart

A balance can sit either side of zero. Columns above and below the line show each year’s surplus or deficit, and as a share of GDP 1950 and 2020 compare fairly.

The indicators

  • Trade balance−£39,447m, cash, 2025 · UK

Read it carefully

This is goods and services together: a long-standing deficit in goods is partly offset by a surplus in services. Trade figures are revised often, and trade in precious metals can swing a single year.
