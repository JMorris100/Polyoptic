Has Britain stopped saving?

No: households saved 9.7% of their disposable income in 2025, against a 9.4% average since 1963.

Source: ONS National Accounts · Open Government Licence v3.0

Open this in the explorer → See it with the other questions

The numbers

Households have saved between 5.1% (2022) and 16.6% (2020) of their disposable income since 1963, 9.4% on average. In 2025 it was 9.7%, close to that average. The highs line up with recessions and the 2020 lockdown, when spending fell — there is no long slide.

Why a line chart

A dashed line at the long-run average turns ‘is it low?’ into something you can see: the ratio crosses it again and again, which is what no trend looks like.

The indicators

  • Household saving ratio9.7% of household disposable income, 2025 · UK

Read it carefully

The saving ratio is what is left of disposable income after spending, so it falls when spending rises as well as when income falls. It is revised heavily, often years later.
