Did lost schooling widen the attainment gap in England?

The gap did widen after the pandemic, from 2.91 in 2018/19 to 3.23 in 2021/22, but these figures cannot show whether lost schooling was the cause.

Source: DfE Explore Education Statistics; DfE pupil absence statistics, via ONS Explore Local Statistics · Open Government Licence v3.0

The claim

Absence since the pandemic has widened the gap between disadvantaged pupils and everyone else.

The numbers

The disadvantage gap index narrowed from 3.34 in 2010/11 to 2.91 in 2018/19, the last year before the pandemic. When it was next measured, in 2021/22, it was 3.23, up 11% on 2018/19, and it is 3.22 in 2025/26. That undoes 71% of the narrowing between 2010/11 and 2018/19. Persistent absence was 10.9% of pupils in 2018/19 and peaked at 22.5% in 2021/22; it is 18.1% in 2024/25. Neither series is measured identically either side of the pandemic: the disadvantage gap index (no KS2 tests 2020, 2021) and persistent absence (recording rules changed in 2020), so compare the before and after figures with care.

What it actually shows

Two lines moving at the same time. It does not show that one caused the other, and nothing on this chart could: absence and attainment share most of their plausible causes, and pupils missing school are disproportionately the same pupils the gap index measures. Read it as a question worth asking of the underlying data, not as an answer.

Why a line chart

Each series is set to 100 in 2018/19, the last year before the pandemic, so the change after it reads the same way for both. The shaded years are the ones the pandemic interrupted.

The indicators

Read it carefully

England only, and academic years, which plot at their starting calendar year — a point labelled 2020 is the 2020/21 school year. Both series changed collection or definition during the pandemic, so the break either side of 2020 is partly a change in measurement.
