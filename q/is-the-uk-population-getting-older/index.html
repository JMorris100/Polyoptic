Is the UK population getting older?

Yes: over-65s are 19.0% of the population, up from 15.8% in 1991, and they now outnumber under-16s.

Source: ONS Explore Local Statistics · Open Government Licence v3.0

Open this in the explorer → See it with the other questions

The numbers

In 1991 under-16s were 20.3% of the UK population and over-65s 15.8%. The over-65s overtook the under-16s in 2020, and by 2024 were 19.0% against 18.2%. The working-age share peaked at 65.1% in 2007 and is 62.8% now.

Why a stacked area chart

The three age groups add up to the whole population, and a stacked area shows that: each band is a share of 100%. Its weakness is that only the bottom band sits on a flat baseline — the moment the over-65s overtake the under-16s is easier to see as a line, one click away in the explorer.

Read it carefully

These are shares, not numbers. The population grew throughout, so a smaller share of under-16s does not have to mean fewer children.
