Are people in the UK living longer — and healthier?

Barely longer than in 2011, and not healthier: healthy life expectancy has fallen to 60.7 years for men and 60.9 for women.

Source: ONS Explore Local Statistics · Open Government Licence v3.0

Open this in the explorer → See it with the other questions

The numbers

Life expectancy at birth rose by 3.1 years for men and 2.3 for women between 2001 and 2011, then all but stopped: by 2022 it was 79.2 for men and 83.0 for women. Healthy life expectancy — the years expected in good health — fell, from 62.7 to 60.7 for men and 63.7 to 60.9 for women. So the years a woman can expect to spend in poor health grew from 19.1 to 22.1.

Why a line chart

All four are in years, so they can honestly share one axis — and the space between each pair of lines is the story: the years lived in poor health.

Read it carefully

Period life expectancy assumes this year’s death rates hold for a whole lifetime: it summarises mortality now rather than forecasting it. Healthy life expectancy rests on people rating their own health in a survey.
