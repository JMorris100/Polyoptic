Questions worth asking

22 questions people actually ask about the UK, answered with the official numbers: each with its chart, the caveats and a link to the working.

Answered in depth · 4
  • Has pay kept up with prices in the UK?Only just, measured from 2008: average pay is 5% ahead of prices, after trailing prices every year from 2009 to 2020. From 2000, it is 24% ahead.
  • Have rents outrun pay in the UK?Not quite, measured from 2015: private rents are 4% behind average pay, having been 8% behind at the widest, in 2022.
  • What does UK government debt cost to carry?4.3% of GDP in 2025: the interest bill is up from 2.7% in 1993, a much smaller rise than the debt’s, which went from 29.7% to 94.2% of GDP.
  • Did lost schooling widen the attainment gap in England?The gap did widen after the pandemic, from 2.91 in 2018/19 to 3.23 in 2021/22, but these figures cannot show whether lost schooling was the cause.
Several trends at once · 8
  • Is the NHS under more strain than before the pandemic?Yes: the waiting list is longer, A&E is slower and ambulances are slower than before the pandemic, and GP numbers have barely changed.
  • Are people in the UK living longer — and healthier?Barely longer than in 2011, and not healthier: healthy life expectancy has fallen to 60.7 years for men and 60.9 for women.
  • Where does UK electricity come from now?Mostly from low-carbon sources: in 2025 renewables made 52.1% of the UK’s electricity, nuclear 12.2% and fossil fuels 32.3%.
  • Is the UK population getting older?Yes: over-65s are 19.0% of the population, up from 15.8% in 1991, and they now outnumber under-16s.
  • Have UK trains, buses and road traffic recovered from lockdown?Rail and road traffic have, buses haven’t: against 2010 = 100, rail 135, road traffic 113 and buses 79.
  • Pay, prices, rents or house prices: what has risen fastest in the UK since 2015?Pay, narrowly: since 2015, pay is up 51%, house prices 47%, private rents 45% and consumer prices 38%.
  • Is UK net migration driven by students or workers?Both: study visas peaked at 624,000 in 2022 and work visas at 614,000 in 2023, and both have fallen since, with net migration down to 171,000 in 2025.
  • Are children in England getting heavier?Older children are: obesity in Year 6 rose from 17.5% to 22.2%, while in reception it is little changed at 10.5%.
A common belief, tested · 6
  • Has crime gone up in England and Wales?Not on the best measure: the Crime Survey shows crime down 65% since 2002-03, though police-recorded crime is up 49% since 2013-14.
  • Is Britain getting unhappier?Barely: average happiness has stayed between 7.29 and 7.56 out of 10 since 2011-12, with a small dip in the pandemic.
  • Are more people dying than ever in England and Wales?No: 571,000 people died in England and Wales in 2025, fewer than in 1918, and within 1% of 1971 although the UK population has grown 24%.
  • Has Britain stopped saving?No: households saved 9.7% of their disposable income in 2025, against a 9.4% average since 1963.
  • Did Brexit tip Britain into a trade deficit?No: the UK ran a trade deficit in every one of the 18 years from 1998 to 2015, before the referendum.
  • Do more police officers mean more crimes charged?Not on these figures: years with more officers have not had higher charge rates — the correlation is −0.60, the wrong way round.
The long view · 4
  • Is England building as many homes as it used to?No: England completed 142,000 homes in 2025, 40% of the 1968 peak of 353,000.
  • When did the UK government last balance the books?In 2001: the government has borrowed every year since, and in 65 of the 78 years from 1948.
  • Are deaths about to overtake births in England and Wales?Not yet, but nearly: births outnumbered deaths in England and Wales by just 14,000 in 2025, against 363,000 at the post-war peak.
  • Did UK productivity stop growing after 2008?Nearly: output per hour grew 2.2% a year from 1971 to 2007, and 0.4% a year since.
