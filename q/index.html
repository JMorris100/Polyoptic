Questions worth asking

22 questions people actually ask about the UK, answered with the official numbers: each with its chart, the caveats and a link to the working.

Answered in depth · 4
  • Did lost schooling widen the attainment gap?The disadvantage gap index narrowed from 3.34 in 2010/11 to 2.91 in 2018/19, the last year before the pandemic.
  • Has pay kept up with prices?Rebased to 2000 = 100, average pay reached 233 by 2025 and prices (CPIH) 188: average pay +133%, prices (CPIH) +88%. Average pay rose faster.
  • Have rents outrun pay?Rebased to 2015 = 100, private rents reached 145 by 2025 and average pay 151: private rents +45%, average pay +51%. Average pay rose faster.
  • What does the debt cost to carry?In 1993 net debt was 29.7% of GDP and the interest on it cost 2.7% of GDP.
Several trends at once · 8
  • Is the NHS under more strain than before the pandemic?The waiting list was 4.6 million in 2019 and 7.3 million in 2025, having peaked at 7.6 million in 2023.
  • Are people in the UK living longer — and healthier?Life expectancy at birth rose by 3.1 years for men and 2.3 for women between 2001 and 2011, then all but stopped: by 2022 it was 79.2 for men and 83.0 for…
  • Where does UK electricity come from now?In 1998 fossil fuels made 68.5% of the UK’s electricity and renewables 2.5%.
  • Is the UK population getting older?In 1991 under-16s were 20.3% of the UK population and over-65s 15.8%. The over-65s overtook the under-16s in 2020, and by 2024 were 19.0% against 18.2%.
  • Have UK trains, buses and road traffic recovered from lockdown?Set each to 2010 = 100. Before the pandemic, in 2018-19, rail journeys stood at 130 and road traffic at 110, while bus journeys had fallen to 93.
  • Pay, prices, rents or house prices: what has risen fastest in the UK since 2015?Rebased to 2015 = 100, by 2025: pay +51%, house prices +47%, private rents +45% and prices (CPIH) +38%. Pay rose most and prices (CPIH) least.
  • Is UK net migration driven by students or workers?Net migration was 184,000 in 2019, peaked at 891,000 in 2022 and was 171,000 in 2025.
  • Are children in England getting heavier?Obesity among reception children was 9.9% in 2006/07 and 10.5% in 2024/25 — little changed. Among Year 6 children it went from 17.5% to 22.2%.
Looks like a trend — isn’t · 6
  • Has crime gone up in England and Wales?Police-recorded crime has risen 49% since its low in 2013-14 — the rise most headlines report.
  • Is Britain getting unhappier?On a scale of 0 to 10, average happiness moved between 7.29 and 7.56 over 12 years — 0.27 of a point.
  • Are more people dying than ever in England and Wales?Deaths in England and Wales have stayed between 445,000 (1923) and 612,000 (1918) every year since 1900. In 2025 there were 571,000.
  • Has Britain stopped saving?Households have saved between 5.1% (2022) and 16.6% (2020) of their disposable income since 1963, 9.4% on average. In 2025 it was 9.7%, close to that average.
  • Did Brexit tip Britain into a trade deficit?The UK ran a trade deficit in every one of the 18 years from 1998 to 2015, before the referendum, and in nine of the 10 years since.
  • Do more police officers mean more crimes charged?Across these 12 years, if anything the years with more officers had lower charge rates (a correlation of −0.41).
The long view · 4
  • Is England building as many homes as it used to?England completed 353,000 new homes in 1968, the most in any year since the war.
  • When did the UK government last balance the books?The government borrowed in 65 of the 78 years from 1948 to 2025. The last surplus was in 2001. Borrowing peaked at 12.7% of GDP in 2020; in 2025 it was 5.0%.
  • Are deaths about to overtake births in England and Wales?In 1947 births outnumbered deaths in England and Wales by 363,000. In 2025 the gap was 14,000: 585,000 births against 571,000 deaths.
  • Did UK productivity stop growing after 2008?Output per hour grew by 2.2% a year on average from 1971 to 2007, and 0.4% a year from 2007 to 2025.
