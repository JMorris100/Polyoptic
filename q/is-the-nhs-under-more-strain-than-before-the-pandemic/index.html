Is the NHS under more strain than before the pandemic?

Yes: the waiting list is longer, A&E is slower and ambulances are slower than before the pandemic, and GP numbers have barely changed.

Source: NHS England Referral to Treatment waiting times; NHS England A&E attendances and emergency admissions; NHS England Ambulance Quality Indicators; NHS England General Practice Workforce statistics · Open Government Licence v3.0 · a year still in progress is left out

The numbers

The waiting list was 4.6 million in 2019 and 7.3 million in 2025, having peaked at 7.6 million in 2023. The share of A&E patients seen within four hours went from 88.0% in 2018-19 to 74.9% in 2025-26. Category 2 ambulances — strokes, heart attacks — averaged 22 minutes in 2018-19, 50 at the worst in 2022-23, and 30 in 2025-26. Fully qualified GPs have barely changed in number: 28,906 full-time equivalents in 2026, against 29,537 in 2016.

Why a small multiples

Four measures in four units — patients, a percentage, minutes, doctors. Small multiples give each its own scale on the same timeline, so you compare the shape and timing of each without pretending they share an axis.

Read it carefully

Every one of these has breaks in it: pathways dropped from the waiting list in 2013 and 2024, A&E trial sites left out from 2019 to 2023, new ambulance standards in 2017. They are marked on the lines in the explorer.
