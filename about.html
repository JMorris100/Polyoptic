About Polyoptic

An honesty layer over official UK statistics. It publishes no numbers of its own — it fetches published ones, puts them on the same axes, and shows you the working.

What it does

Government statistics are published well and presented badly. The numbers are mostly there, in spreadsheets and APIs, but they arrive one release at a time, on different geographies and different year bases, with the caveats in a footnote and the denominator in another document.

Polyoptic fetches 75 series from nine publishers, normalises them into one schema, and puts them on a chart together. Every series carries its coverage, its year basis, its unit and its licence, and the explorer refuses to silently compare across a mismatch — it tells you when you are putting an England series next to a UK one, or an academic year next to a calendar one.

Click any point on a chart and it shows the published figure, the figure being plotted, and the arithmetic in between, with a link to the release it came from. That is the whole idea: a restatement you cannot check is worth no more than the headline it replaced.

What it deliberately does not do

  • It publishes no data of its own. Every value traces to a government release, and Explorer Verify lists the endpoint each one was pulled from and the date it was pulled.
  • It does not correct or model the numbers. Where a figure is contested, the series carries the caveat rather than a fix — the publisher's own wording wherever possible.
  • It does not tell you a rise is good news. Falling emissions and falling life expectancy are not the same story, and a chart does not know which one it is drawing.

Corrections

If a figure here disagrees with the source, the source is right and this is a bug. Do say so — the fetch date and endpoint on Explorer Verify are the fastest way to show what went wrong.
